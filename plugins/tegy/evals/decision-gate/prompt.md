/tegy:review

Original brief: Decide whether a regional waste-hauling company should approve
$5.4 million for 18 new collection trucks now. Missed pickups must fall from 9%
to below 3% within 10 weeks, while operating margin stays at or above 7%.

Decision candidate: Approve all 18 trucks now and begin procurement this week.

Supporting rationale: Missed pickups increased while the fleet's average
utilization reached 82%, so the company has a structural vehicle-capacity
shortage. Eighteen trucks add roughly 20% capacity and should restore service
with a reasonable buffer.

Evidence: Two dense service zones account for 71% of missed pickups, primarily
on Monday mornings. Other zones remain below 2% missed pickups. Utilization in
the affected window reaches 97%, while network-wide utilization averages 82%.
A route-batching rule was changed twelve weeks ago; missed pickups began rising
the following week. Operations restored the old rule in the two affected zones
for a two-week pilot, using the same trucks and crews. Missed pickups fell to
2.5% and operating margin was 7.4%. The truck supplier's earliest delivery is
six months away.

Assumptions and calculations: Eighteen trucks on the current fleet base add
approximately 20% nominal capacity. The candidate assumes the higher capacity
will translate proportionally into fewer missed pickups and that the current
average utilization justifies buying rather than renting.

Alternatives considered: Keep the newer batching rule and rent six trucks for
ten weeks; restore the old batching rule in the affected zones; or combine a
rule rollback with short-term rentals if service deteriorates.

Material unknowns: Long-run maintenance cost of the new trucks, resale value,
and whether demand in the affected zones will remain at the current level after
the next quarter.

Risks and reversal conditions: Procurement can be cancelled only before the
supplier accepts the purchase order. After acceptance, the commitment is not
reversible without a material loss. Management would reconsider only if missed
pickups remain above 3% after the new trucks enter service.
